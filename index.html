EdPal · Learn . Assess . Grow.

Know what you are good at before you choose a course.

A self-evaluation and career-recommendation platform for learners aged 12 to 18 in Kenya. You build a profile, enrol your school subjects, take adaptive assessments and psychometric tests, and get ranked course and career recommendations that follow the KUCCPS model — each one with an explanation.

The problem

Most Kenyan students choose the course they will study for the next four years without any structured information about what they are actually good at.

By the time a learner sits for their final exams, they are usually choosing between clusters they know very little about. Advice comes from whoever is nearby: an older cousin, a neighbour who did the course, sometimes a teacher with forty other students to attend to. None of it is measured against the student's own performance.

KUCCPS then places students by cluster, and the cutoff point for each cluster moves every year. Knowing which cluster your subject combination and grades actually qualify you for is worth more than a list of impressive-sounding courses. That information is public, but it is not in a form a 17-year-old can use the night before they fill in their choices.

How it works

Four steps, in this order. You can stop and come back at any point.

  1. 01

    Create a profile and enrol your subjects

    Your date of birth, your school subjects and the grades you have so far, and up to four career preferences in the order you would actually rank them.

  2. 02

    Take adaptive questionnaires and psychometric tests

    Multiple-choice, ranking, numeric and open-text questions. Attempts are timed but pausable, so you can resume an assessment on another day without losing your answers.

  3. 03

    Get scored the moment you submit

    An overall result, your percentile against other attempts, a pass or fail outcome where the questionnaire sets one, and a breakdown by topic so you can see which parts moved the score.

  4. 04

    See ranked career and course matches

    Each recommendation comes with a written explanation of why it fits you, the subject requirements for the courses behind it, and the cutoff clusters those courses fall into.

What EdPal does

Eight things the platform does today. Each one is something a careers master or a school administrator can check in the product.

Adaptive questionnaires

Multiple choice, ranking, numeric and open-text questions, with time limits and scoring rules you can configure per questionnaire.

Timed, pausable attempts

A learner can start, pause and resume an attempt. On submit it is scored automatically, so nothing waits on manual marking.

Instant scoring with subscores

Overall score, percentile, pass or fail, and a breakdown by topic, calculated the moment an attempt is submitted.

Versioned questionnaires

Editing a questionnaire creates a new version. Past attempts stay linked to the version they were taken on, so reported results do not change under a learner's feet.

Psychometric career tests

A separate assessment type with its own scoring, covering interests and work style alongside the subject-based questionnaires.

KUCCPS-aligned careers and courses

Careers, courses, institutions, subject requirements and cutoff clusters held as structured data and mapped to the KUCCPS course model.

Explained career matches

Recommendations are tagged and ranked, and each one carries a written reason for why it was matched to that learner. Learners can respond to the recommendation; the matching engine is versioned, so a change in the rules is traceable.

Two dashboards: learner and instructor

Learners see their own results, profile and ranked preferences. Instructors get separate tools to create assessments and review attempts.

Where the matching is different

A ranking on its own tells a learner nothing they can act on. EdPal writes down why each career was recommended, then shows the subject requirements and cutoff history behind the courses that lead to it.

Ranking without a reason is a black box Every recommendation in EdPal carries the factors that produced it, in words a learner and a parent can argue with.

Learners can push back A recommendation can be marked as a fit or not. That feedback is stored against the match instead of being discarded.

Rules change, history does not The matching engine is versioned. A later change to the rules does not rewrite what a learner was told last term.

Who it is for

Learners, aged 12 to 18

You build a profile, enrol the subjects you are sitting for school, and work through assessments at your own pace. You see your strengths as scores, then the courses and careers that match them.

Nothing is hidden from you. If EdPal recommends a career, you can read why, what subjects it needs, and what the cutoff has looked like for the cluster it sits in.

  • Percentile and topic subscores on every attempt, so you know where the mark came from.
  • A profile you keep: subjects, grades, and up to four ranked career preferences.
  • Assessments you can pause. Resuming does not mean starting again.

Teachers and schools

You get a separate dashboard: create and version assessments, review attempts, and see how a class performed by topic before you decide what to reteach.

Careers masters can use the same KUCCPS data to answer the question Form 4 leavers actually ask — which cluster do my subjects qualify me for.

Parents and guardians

You see the same evidence the school sees: what your child scored, on what, and which courses those results point to. The recommendation explains itself in plain language, so the conversation at home starts from something concrete.
